Treatment Guides

Diagnosis: Early Blight

Recommended Recovery Plan

Based on your recent scan of Field A-12 (Tomato Crop), we have identified signs of Alternaria solani. Prompt action is required to prevent yield loss.

support_agent Local Expert Support

Connect with Officer Michael Chen for personalized field advice.

psychiatry
1
Prune Infected Leaves

Carefully remove leaves showing dark spots. Sterilize pruning shears between each cut using 70% isopropyl alcohol to avoid cross-contamination.

2
Apply Copper Fungicide

Use an OMRI-listed copper soap. Apply thoroughly to both sides of the leaves every 7-10 days, preferably in the late afternoon to prevent leaf burn.

3
Compost Management

Do not compost infected trimmings. Burn or dispose of them in sealed bags to eliminate fungal spores from your farm's ecosystem.

A
Chlorothalonil Spray

Standard protective fungicide. Apply at the rate of 2.1 lbs per acre. Ensure full coverage. Observe a 7-day pre-harvest interval.

B
Systemic Fungicides

Use Strobilurin-based products (FRAC Group 11) for deeper penetration. Rotate with other modes of action to prevent resistance.

Long-term Preventive Measures

autorenew
Crop Rotation Mastery

Avoid planting solanaceous crops (tomatoes, peppers, potatoes) in the same soil for at least 3 years. Rotate with brassicas or legumes to break the fungal life cycle.

  • check_circle Year 1: Legumes (Beans/Peas)
  • check_circle Year 2: Brassicas (Cabbage/Broccoli)
  • check_circle Year 3: Root Crops (Carrots)
Optimized Irrigation

Switch to drip irrigation. Keeping foliage dry reduces fungal spore activation by 65%.

water_drop
space_dashboard
Airflow Dynamics

Increase plant spacing to 24 inches. Better airflow promotes faster leaf drying after rain.
